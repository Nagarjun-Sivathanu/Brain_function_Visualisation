# Region Summary: Fourth Ventricle

**Region name:** fourth ventricle
**Hierarchy level:** 3
**Parent:** rhombencephalon (hindbrain)
**Type:** CSF-filled cavity of the ventricular system

---

## 1. Overview

The fourth ventricle is the most caudal of the brain's four interconnected, cerebrospinal-fluid-filled ventricles. It sits within the hindbrain, between the brainstem (pons and medulla) anteriorly and the cerebellum posteriorly. It is continuous with the cerebral aqueduct above (connecting to the third ventricle) and with the central canal of the spinal cord below. Its floor (the rhomboid fossa) is diamond-shaped and overlies key brainstem nuclei; its tent-shaped roof projects into the cerebellum. The ventricle is lined by ependyma and contains a choroid plexus that produces cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).

## 2. Detailed Functional Description

- **CSF production.** The choroid plexus of the fourth ventricle (a three-layered structure of vascular endothelium, pia mater, and cuboidal ependyma forming a blood–CSF barrier) filters blood plasma to secrete CSF, contributing to the total CSF volume circulating in the CNS.
- **CSF circulation and outflow.** CSF flows from the third ventricle through the cerebral aqueduct into the fourth ventricle, then exits into the subarachnoid space through three apertures: the midline **foramen of Magendie** (median aperture) into the cisterna magna, and the paired **foramina of Luschka** (lateral apertures) at the cerebellopontine angles. From there CSF bathes the brain and spinal cord and is reabsorbed.
- **Protection and buoyancy.** The CSF it helps produce and channel cushions the brain, provides buoyancy (reducing effective brain weight), and absorbs mechanical shock.
- **Homeostasis and waste clearance.** CSF supports the glymphatic clearance of metabolic waste, maintains a stable chemical environment for neurons, and transports nutrients, hormones, and signalling molecules.
- **Intracranial pressure regulation.** Balanced production, flow, and reabsorption maintain stable intracranial pressure.
- **Anatomical landmark.** Its floor overlies vital brainstem nuclei (e.g., the facial colliculus, vagal and hypoglossal trigones), making it a clinically important structure.

## 3. Behavioural Description

The fourth ventricle has no behaviour of its own; its "behaviour" is the **maintenance of the fluid environment** that every neuron depends on. By producing and routing CSF, it keeps the brain buoyant, protected, chemically stable, and cleared of waste. Obstruction of the fourth ventricle or its outflow apertures causes CSF to back up, raising intracranial pressure and producing hydrocephalus — headache, vomiting, ataxia, and impaired consciousness.

## 4. Functional Mimicry Notes (for agent modelling)

Model the fourth ventricle as a **fluid-management / infrastructure service**: it produces CSF (choroid plexus), routes it (aqueduct in → three apertures out), maintains pressure and chemical homeostasis, and provides cushioning and waste clearance. As an agent it is a support service — not a decision-maker — that other regions depend on for a stable operating environment.
