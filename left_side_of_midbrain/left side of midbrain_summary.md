# Region Summary: Left Side of Midbrain

**Region name:** left side of midbrain
**Hierarchy level:** 3
**Parent:** midbrain (mesencephalon)
**Embedded nuclei (per dataset):** left inferior colliculus, left superior colliculus, left red nucleus, left substantia nigra, brachia of left inferior colliculus, brachia of left superior colliculus

---

## 1. Overview

The left side of the midbrain is the left half of the mesencephalon, containing the left-sided members of the paired mesencephalic nuclei together with the left cerebral peduncle and left tegmentum. As a lateralized unit it organizes visual and auditory reflexes, eye movement, and motor modulation, predominantly serving the **contralateral (right) side of the body and the right visual/auditory hemifield** because major pathways cross the midline. Its embedded nuclei are the left superior and inferior colliculi (with their brachia), the left red nucleus, and the left substantia nigra.

## 2. Detailed Functional Description

- **Visual orienting & gaze (left superior colliculus).** Processes visual input (predominantly from the contralateral/right visual field) and drives reflexive saccades and head/eye orienting toward stimuli on that side, using a retinotopic spatial-attention map. Its **brachium** carries fibres from the optic tract / lateral geniculate to the colliculus.
- **Auditory relay & reflexes (left inferior colliculus).** Principal auditory relay of the left midbrain; receives the lateral lemniscus and projects via its **brachium** to the left medial geniculate body, supporting sound localization and the acoustic startle/orienting reflex.
- **Motor modulation — dopamine (left substantia nigra).** The left substantia nigra pars compacta supplies dopamine to the left basal ganglia, facilitating initiation and scaling of movement (its degeneration drives contralateral-predominant parkinsonian signs); pars reticulata contributes to basal-ganglia output and gaze control.
- **Limb coordination — rubrospinal output (left red nucleus).** Receives cerebellar and cortical input and gives rise to the rubrospinal tract, which **decussates** and influences flexor tone and coordinated movement of the **right** limbs.
- **Eye-movement & pupillary contributions.** Left-sided oculomotor/tegmental circuitry contributes to extraocular movement, pupillary reflexes, and accommodation.
- **Conduction.** The left cerebral peduncle carries descending corticospinal/corticobulbar fibres toward the contralateral body.

## 3. Behavioural Description

The left midbrain's behavioural signature is **lateralized reflexive orienting and motor modulation biased to the opposite (right) side**: snapping the eyes/head toward a light or sound on the right, localizing right-sided sounds, smoothly aiming gaze, and keeping right-limb movement well-scaled and coordinated via dopaminergic and (crossed) rubrospinal output. Left-midbrain lesions characteristically produce contralateral (right-sided) motor deficits and ipsilateral cranial-nerve (e.g., oculomotor) signs — the basis of classic crossed brainstem syndromes (e.g., Weber, Benedikt) when on this side.

## 4. Functional Mimicry Notes (for agent modelling)

Model the left side of midbrain as the **left-hemilateral reflex/motor-modulation unit**: a bundle of left-sided sub-nuclei (superior colliculus = visual orienting; inferior colliculus = auditory relay; substantia nigra = dopaminergic movement facilitation; red nucleus = crossed rubrospinal coordination) that predominantly services the contralateral (right) body/field. It mirrors the right-midbrain agent and shares the midline core ("part of midbrain") substrate.
