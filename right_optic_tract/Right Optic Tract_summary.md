# Region Summary: Right Optic Tract

**Region name:** right optic tract
**Hierarchy level:** 4
**Parent:** diencephalon
**Major components:** —

---

## 1. Overview

The right optic tract is the post-chiasmatic visual pathway carrying the left visual hemifield toward the right thalamus. It is responsible for transmission of the combined left visual hemifield (from both eyes) to the right lateral geniculate nucleus, pretectum, and superior colliculus.

## 2. Detailed Functional Description

- **Carry a hemifield.** After partial decussation at the chiasm, convey the left visual hemifield from both retinae to the right side.
- **Relay to the thalamus.** Project mainly to the right lateral geniculate nucleus for onward conscious vision.
- **Feed reflex centres.** Send collaterals to the pretectum (pupillary reflex) and superior colliculus (gaze/orienting).

## 3. Behavioural Description

A lesion of the right optic tract causes a contralateral (left) homonymous hemianopia affecting both eyes.

## 4. Functional Mimicry Notes (for agent modelling)

Model as a hemifield conduit: a routed cable carrying one half of visual space to its thalamic and reflex targets.
