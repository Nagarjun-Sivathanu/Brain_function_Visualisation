# Region Summary: Right Side of Midbrain

**Region name:** right side of midbrain
**Hierarchy level:** 3
**Parent:** midbrain (mesencephalon)
**Embedded nuclei (per dataset):** right substantia nigra, right red nucleus, right superior colliculus, right inferior colliculus, brachia of right superior colliculus, brachia of right inferior colliculus

---

## 1. Overview

The right side of the midbrain is the right half of the mesencephalon, containing the right-sided members of the paired mesencephalic nuclei together with the right cerebral peduncle and right tegmentum. Like the left half it organizes visual and auditory reflexes, eye movement, and motor modulation — but as a lateralized unit it predominantly serves the **contralateral (left) side of the body and the left visual/auditory hemifield**, owing to the crossing of major pathways. Its embedded nuclei are the right superior and inferior colliculi (with their brachia), the right red nucleus, and the right substantia nigra.

## 2. Detailed Functional Description

- **Visual orienting & gaze (right superior colliculus).** Processes visual input (predominantly from the contralateral/left visual field) and drives reflexive saccades and head/eye orienting toward stimuli on that side; integrates a retinotopic map for spatial attention. Its **brachium** carries fibres from the optic tract / lateral geniculate to the colliculus.
- **Auditory relay & reflexes (right inferior colliculus).** Principal auditory relay of the right midbrain; receives the lateral lemniscus and projects via its **brachium** to the right medial geniculate body, supporting sound localization and the acoustic startle/orienting reflex.
- **Motor modulation — dopamine (right substantia nigra).** The right substantia nigra pars compacta supplies dopamine to the right basal ganglia, facilitating initiation and scaling of movement (clinically, its degeneration drives contralateral-predominant parkinsonian signs); pars reticulata contributes to basal-ganglia output and gaze control.
- **Limb coordination — rubrospinal output (right red nucleus).** Receives cerebellar and cortical input and gives rise to the rubrospinal tract, which **decussates** and influences flexor tone and coordinated movement of the **left** limbs.
- **Eye-movement & pupillary contributions.** Right-sided oculomotor/tegmental circuitry contributes to extraocular movement, pupillary reflexes, and accommodation.
- **Conduction.** The right cerebral peduncle carries descending corticospinal/corticobulbar fibres toward the contralateral body.

## 3. Behavioural Description

The right midbrain's behavioural signature is **lateralized reflexive orienting and motor modulation biased to the opposite (left) side**: snapping the eyes/head toward a light or sound on the left, localizing left-sided sounds, smoothly aiming gaze, and keeping left-limb movement well-scaled and coordinated via dopaminergic and (crossed) rubrospinal output. Right-midbrain lesions characteristically produce contralateral motor deficits and ipsilateral cranial-nerve (e.g., oculomotor) signs — the basis of classic crossed brainstem syndromes (e.g., Weber, Benedikt) when on this side.

## 4. Functional Mimicry Notes (for agent modelling)

Model the right side of midbrain as the **right-hemilateral reflex/motor-modulation unit**: a bundle of right-sided sub-nuclei (superior colliculus = visual orienting; inferior colliculus = auditory relay; substantia nigra = dopaminergic movement facilitation; red nucleus = crossed rubrospinal coordination) that predominantly services the contralateral (left) body/field. It mirrors the left-midbrain agent and shares the midline core ("part of midbrain") substrate.
