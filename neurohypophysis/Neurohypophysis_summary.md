# Region Summary: Neurohypophysis

**Region name:** neurohypophysis
**Hierarchy level:** 5
**Parent:** hypothalamus
**Major components:** posterior pituitary, infundibulum, adenohypophysis (anterior pituitary, associated)

---

## 1. Overview

The neurohypophysis is the posterior pituitary — the hypothalamus's hormone-release terminal. It is responsible for storage and release of hypothalamic hormones (ADH/vasopressin and oxytocin) into the bloodstream.

## 2. Detailed Functional Description

- **Release ADH (vasopressin).** Store and secrete antidiuretic hormone made in the hypothalamus, conserving water by acting on the kidney and raising blood pressure.
- **Release oxytocin.** Secrete oxytocin for uterine contraction, milk let-down, and social bonding.
- **Connect via the infundibulum.** Receive hypothalamic axons through the infundibular stalk; the adjacent adenohypophysis (anterior pituitary) is driven by hypothalamic releasing hormones via the portal system.

## 3. Behavioural Description

Loss of ADH release causes central diabetes insipidus (excess dilute urine, thirst); the pituitary is the master endocrine output of the hypothalamic axes.

## 4. Functional Mimicry Notes (for agent modelling)

Model as the hormone-release terminal of the hypothalamus: it stores and emits ADH/oxytocin on command.
