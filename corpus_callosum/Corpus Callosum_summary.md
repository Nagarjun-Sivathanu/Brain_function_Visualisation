# Region Summary: Corpus Callosum

**Region name:** corpus callosum
**Hierarchy level:** 4
**Parent:** telencephalon
**Latin / meaning:** "tough body"
**Major components:** rostrum, genu, body, splenium

---

## 1. Overview

The corpus callosum is the great commissure bridging the two cerebral hemispheres. It is responsible for the largest white-matter tract in the brain, transferring information between the left and right hemispheres.

## 2. Detailed Functional Description

- **Connect the hemispheres.** Carry ~200 million axons that share sensory, motor, and cognitive information between corresponding cortical areas of the two hemispheres.
- **Integrate the two sides.** Allow unified perception and bimanual coordination by keeping the hemispheres in register.
- **Balance lateralised function.** Let dominant-hemisphere functions (e.g., language) communicate with the other side.

## 3. Behavioural Description

Severing it (callosotomy / 'split-brain') leaves each hemisphere acting semi-independently — e.g., the right hand may not 'know' what the left is doing, and verbal report (left hemisphere) cannot describe left-visual-field stimuli.

## 4. Functional Mimicry Notes (for agent modelling)

Model as the high-bandwidth bus between the two hemisphere processors; it transfers, it does not compute.
