# Region Summary: Telencephalon (Cerebrum)

**Region name:** telencephalon
**Hierarchy level:** 3
**Parent:** prosencephalon (forebrain)
**Synonyms:** cerebrum, endbrain
**Major components:** cerebral cortex (two hemispheres; frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital, limbic/insular lobes), subcortical white matter, basal ganglia, hippocampus, amygdala, olfactory structures; contains the lateral ventricles

---

## 1. Overview

The telencephalon (cerebrum) is the largest and most rostral, most evolutionarily advanced part of the brain. It develops from the dorsal telencephalon (pallium → cerebral cortex) and the ventral telencephalon (subpallium → basal ganglia). It comprises the two cerebral hemispheres — each with an outer folded layer of grey matter (the cerebral cortex, with gyri and sulci that maximize surface area) over white-matter tracts — plus deep subcortical nuclei (basal ganglia) and limbic structures (hippocampus, amygdala). It is the seat of conscious experience, voluntary action, language, memory, and higher cognition.

## 2. Detailed Functional Description

- **Sensory perception.** Primary sensory cortices (somatosensory in parietal, visual in occipital, auditory in temporal) and association areas build conscious perception from thalamic relays.
- **Voluntary motor control.** Primary motor and premotor cortices plan and command voluntary movement; together with the basal ganglia they select, initiate, scale, and sequence motor programs and suppress unwanted ones.
- **Executive function & cognition.** Prefrontal cortex supports attention, working memory, reasoning, planning, decision-making, impulse control, and abstraction.
- **Language.** Dominant-hemisphere networks (Broca's area for production, Wernicke's area for comprehension) and their connections enable speech and language.
- **Memory & learning.** The hippocampus encodes and consolidates declarative memory; cortico-striatal loops underpin habit and procedural learning; cortical plasticity stores long-term knowledge and skills.
- **Emotion & motivation.** The amygdala and limbic/cingulate cortex assign emotional value, drive fear/reward responses, and regulate social-emotional behaviour.
- **Olfaction.** Unique among senses, smell projects directly to telencephalic olfactory cortex without an obligatory thalamic relay.
- **Integration & self.** Association cortices integrate across modalities to support spatial awareness, the body schema, self-reflection, and social cognition.

## 3. Behavioural Description

The telencephalon's behavioural signature is **everything we recognize as the conscious mind**: seeing and recognizing objects and faces, speaking and understanding language, remembering experiences, reasoning and planning, feeling and regulating emotions, choosing and executing deliberate actions, and forming a sense of self and others. Cerebral damage produces region-specific deficits — paralysis, sensory loss, aphasia, amnesia, agnosia, neglect, executive dysfunction, and personality change.

## 4. Functional Mimicry Notes (for agent modelling)

Model the telencephalon as the **top cognitive/executive layer** with specialized cortical-lobe sub-agents (frontal = executive/motor/language-production; parietal = somatosensation/spatial; temporal = auditory/language-comprehension/memory; occipital = vision; limbic = emotion/memory) plus a basal-ganglia action-selection unit and a hippocampal memory unit. It receives relayed input from the diencephalon, constructs perception, reasons, and issues voluntary goals.
